Case Study

McCusker Institute
Designing the Systems Behind a Research Institute's Student Journey

Two internship placements building the workflow automation and web infrastructure behind an ANU research institute's day-to-day operations

Role
Computing Intern (two placements)
Workflow automation, UX research & front-end web design
Timeline
Phase 01: Jul – Oct 2025
Phase 02: Feb – May 2026
Deliverables
Automated newsletter system, redesigned staff directory & homepage, site-wide IA audit, redesigned program page

Summary

Across two placements as a Computing Intern at ANU's McCusker Institute, I first automated the institute's weekly newsletter workflow using SharePoint and Power Automate, then returned a semester later to lead a broader website redesign — auditing the site's information architecture and redesigning its homepage, staff directory, and main program page to fix a confusing, low-conversion user journey.

Background & Challenge

A research institute
run on manual processes

The McCusker Institute (hosted by Michelle Cheah and Chris Browne) connects academic research at ANU with real-world social issues, equipping students to address complex problems through cross-disciplinary, project-based learning.

Like many small institutes, its day-to-day operations relied heavily on manual work: staff were spending time on repetitive administrative tasks instead of teaching, the institute's external-facing presence was hard to navigate, and information about its programs was scattered and unclear. My task across both placements was to design systems — both behind the scenes and on the website itself — that reduced that manual load and made the institute easier to engage with.

Phase 01 · Jul – Oct 2025

Automating the Newsletter Distribution Process

The institute's weekly newsletter — announcing upcoming KNoT sessions (its term for interactive workshops) — was being assembled and sent by hand every week. I designed and built an automated workflow using SharePoint and Power Automate that removed that manual step entirely.

Recurrence

The workflow triggers automatically on a recurring schedule (every Friday at 9 AM), so newsletters go out consistently without manual intervention.

Retrieve & Parse

Pulls the preprocessed event dataset from SharePoint and parses it into structured data — title, start time, session slug, location — for filtering.

Combine into HTML

Filters events to the following week, then dynamically generates an HTML block per event, merged into one styled newsletter body.

Send

Embeds the finished HTML into an email template and sends it via Outlook to the relevant student or staff mailing list.

Once the workflow was live, I benchmarked our approach against two alternatives, to make sure I was recommending the right long-term direction rather than just the fastest one to ship.

I built the MVP on the ANU-supported tier (SharePoint + Power Automate) for a fast, low-cost rollout within the internship's timeframe — while documenting the fully custom approach as the recommended path if the institute's usage grows past roughly 200 users.

Phase 02 · Feb – May 2026

Website Redesign & Information Architecture

The institute invited me back the following semester for a second placement, this time focused on the public-facing website — its structure, its staff directory, its homepage, and the program page prospective students land on.

GivePulse Platform Evaluation

Should we build, or should we adopt?

Before redesigning anything, I evaluated GivePulse — a widely-adopted U.S. university platform — against the institute's needs, assessing its maturity, event-management features (eligibility screening, milestone tracking), and impact-tracking capabilities to inform a build-vs-adopt recommendation.

Homepage News & Events Slider

Making recent activity visible on arrival

Designed and implemented a homepage slider surfacing recent KNoT sessions and upcoming events, so visitors see the institute's current activity the moment they land on the site, rather than digging for it.

Staff Directory Redesign

Enhancing structure, clarity, and interactivity

Grouped members into "Institute Staff" and "Fellows & Affiliates" to clarify organisational structure, added role and background tags to each card, and implemented hover effects that signal clickability through to detailed profile pages.

Site-Wide Information Architecture Audit

Mapping the whole site before touching a single page

Before redesigning individual pages, I mapped the entire site structure in a single spreadsheet — every label, button, and destination — to identify duplicate content and confusing user flows, and turned that audit into concrete restructuring suggestions.

Study Page Redesign

From confusing to a clear four-step story

The audit identified the "Study" page — the page prospective students land on — as the site's weakest link: an unclear program structure, no clear value proposition, and key information buried several clicks deep.

I redesigned it around a clear four-step program flow, brought key terms and steps onto a single page with optional "read more" detail, and clarified exactly what students would do, learn, and achieve.

Outcome & Reflection

What two placements
added up to

Across both semesters, the throughline was the same: find where manual, unclear, or undocumented processes were costing the institute time or clarity, then design a system — whether a workflow, a page, or an information architecture — to fix it.

Insight

Evaluated the GivePulse platform and audited the full site's content to find where clarity was breaking down

Innovation

Redesigned the site's layout and automated its newsletter workflow to improve usability end to end

Collaboration

Worked with my supervisor using Git, aligning on requirements and dividing development work

Adoption

Took part in institute events to deepen my understanding of the programs I was designing for

Looking ahead, the institute and I have identified further opportunities — refining the UI further, adding category-based filtering, building portals for future partners, and having me mentor the next intake of interns.
